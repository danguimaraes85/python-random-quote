## Key Question 1 — What does each section (Verse 1, hook, Verse 2, Verse 3 by Rappin' 4-Tay, outro) literally say, and what does Genius say it means?

### Takeaway
No Genius annotation text could be retrieved (host blocked), so the "what Genius says" half of this question is unanswered. Secondary sources reachable only through search summaries agree on the song's broad arc — a defiant refusal of human judgment, paranoia/mistrust of friends, a near-death hospital scene tied to the 1994 shooting, and a closing guest verse by Rappin' 4-Tay — but none of them is Genius, and none could be read in full.

### Cited Findings
- The track is "Only God Can Judge Me" by 2Pac feat. Rappin' 4-Tay, track 10 of *All Eyez on Me*, running 4:56 — [Album of the Year song page](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/song/72363-only-god-can-judge-me/); the bootleg compilation *The Prophet: The Best of the Works* also lists it at 4:56 with Rappin' 4-Tay — [Wikipedia: The Prophet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prophet:_The_Best_of_the_Works).
- A POW Magazine ranking of 2Pac songs (found via search snippet; page itself blocked) says "much of the song is him reflecting, in unnerving detail, on the 1994 Quad Studios shooting and the post-traumatic stress he suffered in its aftermath," and that "a vital signs monitor beeps in the background of his second verse" — [POW MAG, The 50 Greatest 2Pac Songs](https://www.powmag.net/p/the-50-greatest-2pac-songs) (snippet only; rank number not visible).
- Song Meanings and Facts (fan-analysis site, snippet only) describes the track as "an ode to the idea that it is only the judgment of a Higher Power that truly matters," says a verse "presented as being fictional in nature" actually alludes to "an assassination attempt that was made on Tupac's life in 1994," describes the second verse as dealing with "a life-threatening injury, and his fear of dying," and calls the final (Rappin' 4-Tay) verse "a celebration of the thug life" — [Song Meanings and Facts](https://songmeaningsandfacts.com/only-god-can-judge-me-by-2pac-ft-rappin-4-tay/).
- Other meaning-sites (low quality, snippets only) summarize the song as 2Pac feeling "constantly being judged by society" and holding that "only God has the right to judge him," plus "mistrust for people, including his friends who have betrayed him, and his fear of the future" — [Musician Wages](https://www.musicianwages.com/the-meaning-behind-the-song-only-god-can-judge-me-by-2pac/); [Songtell](https://www.songtell.com/2pac-ft-rappin-4-tay/only-god-can-judge-me). One of them calls the song's themes "trust, betrayal, paranoia, self-reflection, and the struggles faced by individuals in marginalized communities" — [Lyrics Meanings](https://lyricsmeanings.com/2pac-only-god-can-judge-me).
- Behind the Rhymes' top-50 list calls the song "a defiant anthem" — [Behind the Rhymes](https://beats-rhymes-lists.beehiiv.com/p/top-50-best-2pac-songs-time); it also appears in Far Out Magazine's and Hip Hop Hero's 50-best lists (positions differ by list) — [Far Out](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/50-best-2pac-songs-of-all-time-playlist/); [Hip Hop Hero](https://hiphophero.com/2pacs-50-greatest-songs-of-all-time/).
- Lyric transcriptions on third-party sites include the heart-monitor cues as bracketed stage directions in the text (per search summary; pages blocked) — [LyricsTranslate](https://lyricstranslate.com/en/2Pac-Only-God-can-judge-me-lyrics.html); [AbsoluteLyrics](http://www.absolutelyrics.com/lyrics/view/2pac/only_god_can_judge_me); [DamnLyrics](https://damnlyrics.com/lyrics/Only-God-Can-Judge-Me-2Pac-ghWDQWJj.html).

### Inferences
- Section map that the writer can use as a scaffold, **UNVERIFIED (background knowledge, could not be checked against Genius or any lyric page)**: an Intro in which 2Pac addresses listeners with "Only God can judge me" / "that right?" ad-libs; **Verse 1** opening "Perhaps I was blind to the facts, stabbed in the back" and the well-known refrain-like couplet about picturing paradise ("Picture paradise...") and the fear-of-reincarnation line ("My only fear of death is reincarnation") plus "They say it's the white man I should fear / but it's my own kind doin' all the killin' here"; **Hook**: repetition of "Only God can judge me" with "nobody else" responses; **Verse 2**: the hospital tableau ("I hear the doctor standing over me, screaming I can make it"), heart-monitor beeps, and the line about being shot ("I'd rather die like a man than live like a coward" also sits in this stretch in most transcriptions), "Pay the devil" imagery, and prison/snitch references; **Verse 3** (Rappin' 4-Tay): a Bay Area–inflected verse about surviving, "five-O" (police), getting paid, and loyalty to 2Pac; **Outro**: 2Pac's spoken shout-outs/"Only God can judge me" repeats. Each of these quotes is under 90 characters and each must be verified against genius.com before being used as a quotation.
- Given the POW Mag and Song Meanings descriptions (both independently mention the monitor/near-death verse and the 1994 shooting), it is reasonable to infer that Genius's community annotations on Verse 2 connect the hospital lines to the Quad Studios shooting — but this is an inference, not a reading of Genius.
- The fact that Song Meanings characterizes 4-Tay's verse as "a celebration of the thug life" suggests the verse's slang register (thug, homie, five-O) is what Portuguese translators would have had to gloss — see KQ4.

## Key Question 2 — Which biographical events do the annotations connect specific lines to?

### Takeaway
Genius's own line-to-event mapping is unavailable, but reachable commentary (POW Mag, Song Meanings and Facts, Rappin' 4-Tay interviews) connects the song to the 30 Nov 1994 Quad Studios shooting (Verse 2's hospital/monitor passage), to 2Pac's 1995 incarceration (origin story of the 4-Tay collaboration), and to the autumn-1995 Death Row/Can-Am sessions that followed his release on bail.

### Cited Findings
- **Quad Studios shooting (30 Nov 1994):** 2Pac "was scheduled to record a verse with Little Shawn at Quad Studios in Manhattan to help pay his legal fees"; "three armed men beat, shot, and robbed" him in the lobby; afterward he implied in a Vibe interview with Kevin Powell that Biggie, Puff Daddy and Andre Harrell were involved; Dexter Isaac later claimed James Rosemond orchestrated it, and no one was charged — [FOX 5 NY](https://www.fox5ny.com/news/quad-studios-shooting-tupac-shakur-attack-manhattan); [Wikipedia: East Coast–West Coast rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast%E2%80%93West_Coast_hip-hop_rivalry); [Oddstops: Quad Studios location](https://oddstops.com/quad-studios-location).
- **"Shot five times" dispute:** Jeff Pearlman's 2025 biography *Only God Can Judge Me: The Many Lives of Tupac Shakur* reports it was three shots, one possibly self-inflicted, challenging the common "five shots" account; publicly 2Pac "insisted he had been shot five times and was the victim of a targeted hit" — [Yahoo Entertainment on Pearlman's book](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/music/articles/tupac-secret-injury-lie-may-003043477.html); see also [Soul In Stereo interview with Pearlman](https://soulinstereo.com/2025/10/a-convo-with-jeff-pearlman-author-of-only-god-can-judge-me-the-many-lives-of-tupac-shakur.html) and [NPR transcript](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5398069) (the latter places Tupac at Clinton Correctional Facility in Dannemora, NY, and discusses Suge Knight posting his bail).
- **Prison (1995):** 2Pac "was convicted of sexual assault in 1994 and served eight months in prison, but was released pending appeal in 1995" — [Wikipedia: Murder of Tupac Shakur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Tupac_Shakur) (snippet).
- **Song's origin in the prison period:** Song Meanings and Facts, citing a Rappin' 4-Tay interview, says "the origin of this song can ultimately be traced back to him showing Tupac love while he was incarcerated in 1994," that 4-Tay "did indeed send Tupac Shakur some gifts while the latter was incarcerated in New York, since the two of them were lifelong friends," and that after Pac's release "they unexpectedly ran into each other in Los Angeles" — [Song Meanings and Facts](https://songmeaningsandfacts.com/only-god-can-judge-me-by-2pac-ft-rappin-4-tay/). A separate Chad Kiser interview has 4-Tay describing a pattern where "I would be free and 'Pac would be locked up, then I would be locked up and Pac would be free," and placing their reconnection in the Bay Area right after Pac "had just signed with Death Row" — [Chad Kiser Q&A with Rappin' 4-Tay (2011)](https://chadkiser.com/2011/08/01/qa-with-rappin-4-tay-legend-talks-still-standing-album-reminisces-about-career-the-ballerstatus-interview/). The two accounts differ on setting (LA vs Bay Area).
- **Recording:** *All Eyez on Me* was recorded 13 Oct – 18 Dec 1995 at Can-Am Studios, Tarzana, and released 13 Feb 1996, "just seven months before his death" — [Wikipedia: All Eyez on Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Eyez_on_Me) (snippet). A fan upload claims the original version was cut at Death Row's Can-Am Studios "on this day in 1995" with Rappin' 4-Tay (user-written description, unverified date) — [YouTube: Original Version HQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CuQGi49D6bY).
- **Credits:** produced by Doug Rasheed and Harold "Scrap" Freddie (2PacLegacy spells it "Fretty," credited to "The Bullets Production Team"); writers 2Pac and Doug Rasheed; guest vocals Rappin' 4-Tay; drums sampled from Audio Two's "Top Billin'" (1987, prod. Daddy-O/Audio Two), sample audible from 0:15 — [WhoSampled song page](https://www.whosampled.com/2Pac/Only-God-Can-Judge-Me/); [WhoSampled sample page](https://www.whosampled.com/sample/5094/2Pac-Rappin'-4-Tay-Only-God-Can-Judge-Me-Audio-Two-Top-Billin'/); [2PacLegacy track page](https://2paclegacy.net/10-2pac-only-god-can-judge-me-all-eyez-on-me-book-1/). Doug Rasheed has given a video interview on how the song came together — [YouTube: Doug Rasheed Interview Part 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h3EoiSwdNt4).
- **Legacy for 4-Tay:** Wikipedia says 4-Tay "was featured on 2Pac's All Eyez on Me album on the track 'Only God Can Judge Me' in 1996" and that his mainstream success "has been scarce since then" — [Wikipedia: Rappin' 4-Tay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rappin%27_4-Tay); CBS News calls the collaboration "a career-defining moment, one he remembers with reverence" — [CBS News Bay Area](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/rappin-4tay-uplifts-youth-while-battling-cancer). 4-Tay's Wikipedia article also mentions an out-of-court settlement in which he "was promised $100,000 and future credits" for a song, but the snippet does not clearly tie this to "Only God Can Judge Me" — [Wikipedia: Rappin' 4-Tay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rappin%27_4-Tay) (treat as unconfirmed for this track).

### Inferences
- Because the recording window (Oct–Dec 1995) falls weeks after 2Pac's release on bail (Oct 1995), the hospital/monitor verse is almost certainly a retrospective dramatization of the Nov 1994 shooting written during/after the prison stay — consistent with POW Mag's "post-traumatic stress" framing. Whether Genius annotators say the same could not be checked.
- The "shot five times" line (UNVERIFIED wording) is likely annotated on Genius in light of the Pearlman three-shot claim only if the annotation was updated after 2025; older community annotations would simply repeat the five-shot narrative.
