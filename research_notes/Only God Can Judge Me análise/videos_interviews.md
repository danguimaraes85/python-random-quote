## Key Question 1 — What did Rappin' 4-Tay say about the session, writing his verse, and his relationship with Tupac?

### Takeaway
Rappin' 4-Tay's most detailed on-record account is a 2011 Q&A with Bay Area journalist Chad Kiser: he says he and Tupac had known each other for years in the Bay Area ("like a brother thing"), ran into each other right after Pac signed to Death Row, and 4-Tay proposed a record deliberately unrelated to the East–West feud, for which he "had the right beat"; they then went to the Death Row studio and "put that song together." A secondary fan site adds an unverified story that 4-Tay sent Tupac gifts while he was in prison in New York and bumped into him in L.A. after his release. No VladTV or Art of Dialogue interview with 4-Tay about this song surfaced in any search.

### Cited Findings
- **Chad Kiser, "Conversations With Chad: Rappin 4-Tay Talks 'Still Standing' Album, Ant Banks & TWDY, Working With 2Pac + More," 1 Aug 2011 (originally a BallerStatus interview).** Snippet wording: "Well you see, me and 'Pac met years ago in the Bay Area... it's always been like a brother thing" **(direct quote per snippet)**. He describes a pattern where one of them would be free while the other was locked up, and says that one time both were free and he saw Tupac when Pac "had just signed with Death Row." He told Tupac he wanted a song that had "nothing to do with" the East Coast–West Coast rivalry and that he had the right beat for them; "So we went down there to the studio and put that song together" **(direct quote per snippet)**, and it became a hit. He also says Tupac "had many plans for the Bay Area and the West Coast." — [Chad Kiser](https://chadkiser.com/2011/08/01/qa-with-rappin-4-tay-legend-talks-still-standing-album-reminisces-about-career-the-ballerstatus-interview/)
- **Song Meanings and Facts (fan/secondary site)** relays a 4-Tay account that he "showed Tupac love while he was incarcerated in 1994," sending gifts to Tupac in prison in New York, and that the two "unexpectedly ran into each other in Los Angeles" after Tupac's release. The site does not name the original interview. It also states this was the only track the two released together during Tupac's lifetime, and credits the song to Tupac and 4-Tay "with additional input from its producers, D. Rasheed and H.S. Freddie." **Treat as unverified secondary lore.** — [Song Meanings and Facts](https://songmeaningsandfacts.com/only-god-can-judge-me-by-2pac-ft-rappin-4-tay/)
- **CBS News Bay Area / KPIX "Bay Area Beat," 26 Jul 2025 (also syndicated to KEYT, ABC17):** profile of 4-Tay mentoring youth at an Oakland boxing program while battling cancer; he received a Lifetime Achievement Award at the History of the Bay Day Festival. He describes the 1996 collaboration with Tupac on "Only God Can Judge Me" as a career-defining moment he looks back on "with reverence," and of Tupac: "there's a chemistry that I just don't think I'll ever be able to experience that again" **(direct quote per snippet)**. — [CBS News SF](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/rappin-4tay-uplifts-youth-while-battling-cancer); [KEYT](https://keyt.com/news/2025/07/26/rap-icon-rappin-4tay-uplifts-youth-while-battling-cancer-bay-area-beat/)
- Wikipedia (Rappin' 4-Tay) confirms he was "featured on 2Pac's All Eyez on Me album on the track 'Only God Can Judge Me' in 1996." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rappin%27_4-Tay)
- Songwriting credits list Anthony Forté (4-Tay's legal name) alongside Tupac Shakur, Douglas B. Rasheed and Harold A. Fretty, which supports that 4-Tay wrote his own verse. — [Tupac.be credits](https://tupac.be/en/his-work/albums/all-eyez-on-me/?s=only-god-can-judge-me); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Only_God_Can_Judge_Me)
- A 2023 page reports that royalties for the song were put up for auction with Anthony Forté listed as songwriter (low-reliability source, flagged in search). — reported via [search summary of the Wikipedia/Soundcharts results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rappin%27_4-Tay)
- A "YFH Chat With Rappin 4-Tay" exists at yourfriendshouse.com but its content on Tupac could not be retrieved. — [Your Friends House](https://yourfriendshouse.com/uncategorised/yfh-chat-with-rappin-4-tay/)
- POW Mag's "50 Greatest 2Pac Songs" argues the song's "smooth feel comes largely from Rappin' 4-Tay's verse, which offers Pac counsel and support." — [POW Mag](https://www.powmag.net/p/the-50-greatest-2pac-songs)

### Inferences
- The 2011 Kiser account is the only first-person, dated, journalist-conducted 4-Tay interview found; the "gifts in prison" story is only attested through a fan site and should be labeled as unverified lore in the article.
- Two slightly different origin stories coexist (chance meeting in L.A. after Pac's release vs. seeing him right after the Death Row signing); they are compatible (Pac signed with Death Row while still in Clinton Correctional and was released Oct 1995, then went straight to Can-Am), but the writer should not merge them into one authoritative narrative.
- The Forté songwriting credit plus 4-Tay's "I had the right beat" remark suggest 4-Tay brought in the Rasheed/Freddie track idea and wrote his own verse — but 4-Tay's claim conflicts with Doug Rasheed's account (see KQ2) that the beat reached Tupac via Rasheed's brother. The two accounts may both be partially true; flag the conflict.

### Gaps
- No VladTV, The Art of Dialogue, or podcast appearance by 4-Tay discussing this specific song was found in any search; either they don't exist or are not indexed with this title.
- No 4-Tay statement about the Can-Am studio by name, how long his verse took, or whether Tupac directed the verse content was found.
- The full Kiser Q&A could not be opened; only the snippet above is confirmed.

## Key Question 2 — What have the producers (Doug Rasheed, Harold "Scrap" Freddie) said about the production?

### Takeaway
Doug Rasheed has given at least three on-record accounts: a two-part video interview for 2pac-forum.com (posted 23 Jan 2015: Part 1 "How 'Only God Can Judge Me' Came Together & All Eyez On Me Album"; Part 2 "Breakdown of the Classic 2Pac Song"), a Charlotte TV news interview (c. autumn 2023) in which he says he gave a demo to his brother, who was friends with Tupac, and Tupac then called wanting to write to two tracks that became "Only God Can Judge Me" and "Ratha Be Ya Nigga," and a RapIndustry.com interview where he says Tupac told him the song "was going to be the third single." Nothing from Harold "Scrap" Freddie (credited as Harold A. Fretty) was found, and no producer statement about the heartbeat/EKG sound was found.

### Cited Findings
- **2pac-forum.com / makaveliforum, 23 Jan 2015 (X post linking YouTube):** "Doug Rasheed Interview About: How The Song Only God Can Judge Me' Came Together & All Eyez On Me Album" **(title per snippet)**. — [X post](https://x.com/makaveliforum/status/558740441609564160)
- **YouTube, "Doug Rasheed Interview Part 1: How 'Only God Can Judge Me' Came Together & All Eyez On Me Album"** — listing text says he "Produced Only God Can Judge Me & Rather Be Ya N.I.G.G.A For 2Pac" **(per snippet)**. Content not retrievable. — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h3EoiSwdNt4)

Fretty. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Only_God_Can_Judge_Me); [Tupac.be](https://tupac.be/en/his-work/albums/all-eyez-on-me/?s=only-god-can-judge-me)
- **Sample:** WhoSampled lists a sample of Audio Two's "Top Billin'" (1987). — [WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/sample/5094/2Pac-Rappin'-4-Tay-Only-God-Can-Judge-Me-Audio-Two-Top-Billin'/)

### Inferences
- Rasheed's "third single" quote is notable precisely because the song never became a single — useful journalistic detail (Tupac's death in Sept 1996 is the obvious explanation, but no source states that).
- Rasheed's "six months before the murder" timing (≈ March 1996) conflicts with the album's recording window (Oct–Dec 1995); he is probably compressing time, or referring to when the record was released/promoted. Flag as a chronological inconsistency.
- Rasheed's demo-via-brother story and 4-Tay's "I had the right beat" story are not reconciled by any source.

### Gaps
- No interview, quote or profile of Harold "Scrap" Freddie / Harold A. Fretty was found at all.
- No source explains the origin of the heartbeat/EKG/"flatline" sound design.
- The 2015 two-part video interview — the single richest primary source — could not be watched or transcribed in this session; the writer should watch it directly.
- No comments from Daz Dillinger, Kurupt, Johnny "J," Dave Aron or Tommy D specifically about this track were found; Dave Aron is confirmed as engineer/mixer on *All Eyez on Me* generally, and Johnny J is credited with 11 of the album's tracks. — [Wikipedia, Dave Aron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Aron); [The Root](https://theroot.com/tupac-and-the-producers-of-the-classic-all-eyez-on-me-1790902506)

## Key Question 3 — Which Tupac interview quotes (with date and outlet) best explain the lyrics' themes (faith, God, judgment, paranoia, Quad Studios)?

### Takeaway
The most directly relevant on-record statements come from Tupac's Vibe interviews with Kevin Powell — the Rikers Island "Ready to Live" interview (conducted Jan 1995, published April 1995), in which he first implied Biggie, Puffy and Andre Harrell knew about or were behind the Nov 1994 Quad Studios shooting, and a 1996 interview (published posthumously by Vibe/Okayplayer in 2018) in which he says "I don't have no fear of death. My only fear is coming back reincarnated." *Tupac: Resurrection* (2003) opens with his own voice saying of being shot, "I'm surprised, but I'm happy, because I believe it's all in God's hands." The famous "God blesses those that hustle" line could not be sourced to a dated interview.

In it he "implied that Biggie, Puff Daddy and Uptown Records head Andre Harrell were involved in or responsible for the attack" at Quad Studios. — [2PacLegacy transcript](https://2paclegacy.net/tupac-interview-kevin-powell-for-vibe-magazine/); [Wikipedia, East Coast–West Coast rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast%E2%80%93West_Coast_hip-hop_rivalry)

### Inferences
- The lyric's hospital/paranoia imagery (doctor over the bed, "flatline," not trusting those around him) maps directly onto Tupac's own public account of Quad Studios in the Jan 1995 Vibe interview and the 1995 Rikers audio — these are the best primary anchors for a lyrical analysis.
- Tupac's "no fear of death / fear of reincarnation" and "all in God's hands" statements support reading the hook as fatalistic faith rather than defiance of consequences, matching the interpretation in Lyreka's analysis (see KQ4).
- Dates matter: the Soren MTV interview is 27 Oct 1995 (during the Can-Am sessions), not 1996; the Powell "paranoid" interview is 1996 but was not published until 2018.

### Gaps
- Could not verify any Tupac quote that uses the exact phrase "only God can judge me" in an interview (quote sites attribute it only to the song title / *The Rose That Grew From Concrete*, and one flags it as unreliably sourced). — [Quotepark](https://quotepark.com/quotes/1149073-tupac-shakur-only-god-can-judge-me-that-which-does-not-kill-me/)
- No Rolling Stone 1996 interview quote on faith was found.
- No Sway interview quote relevant to this song surfaced.
- The 2paclegacy.net transcript could not be opened to confirm exact wording of any 1995 Vibe quote.

## Key Question 5 — How has the song been used in film, TV, theater, documentaries and live performance?

### Takeaway
Confirmed uses: the Broadway musical *Holler If Ya Hear Me* (Palace Theatre, 19 Jun – 20 Jul 2014) placed "Only God Can Judge Me" in Act II; the film *The Hate U Give* (2018) uses it diegetically — Khalil plays it in his car and explains "Thug Life" to Starr just before the police stop in which he is killed — and it is track 4 (4:56) on the Def Jam soundtrack (12 Oct 2018). The song was **not** performed at the 4 July 1996 House of Blues show (setlist.fm lists 10 songs, none of them this one), is **not** on the *Gang Related* soundtrack (1997), and no evidence was found of its use in *Tupac: Resurrection*, *Dear Mama*, *Thug Angel*, *Biggie & Tupac* or the 2017 biopic.
