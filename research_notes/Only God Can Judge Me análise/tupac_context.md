## Key Question 1: What exactly happened in the Quad Recording Studios shooting (Nov. 30, 1994) and the verdict the next day?

### Takeaway
Shortly after midnight on November 30, 1994, Tupac was robbed and shot in the lobby of Quad Recording Studios in Times Square, Manhattan, by three armed men; the dominant account (The Source, XXL, Crime Library, NYT as reported by Rolling Stone Brasil) is five gunshot wounds (head, groin, hand), roughly $40,000 in jewelry taken, emergency surgery at Bellevue, self-discharge within hours against medical advice, and a bandaged, wheelchair-bound appearance in court the next day (Dec. 1, 1994) when the jury returned a guilty verdict on sexual-abuse counts. He later accused Sean "Puffy" Combs and the Notorious B.I.G. of complicity or foreknowledge; no one was ever charged.

### Cited Findings
- Date/place: the shooting occurred November 30, 1994, at Quad Recording Studios in Manhattan; Tupac and his entourage arrived shortly after midnight; he had come to record with Little Shawn — [The Source, "Today in Hip-Hop History: Tupac Shakur Shot 5 Times at Quad Studios" (2019)](https://thesource.com/2019/11/30/today-in-hip-hop-history-tupac-shakur-shot-5-times-at-quad-studios/); [XXL, "Today in Hip-Hop: 2Pac Shot at Quad Studios"](https://www.xxlmag.com/today-in-hip-hop-2pac-shot-at-quad-studios-in-new-york-city/); [Substack: The Quad Recording Studios Incident](https://shamarieknight.substack.com/p/the-quad-recording-studios-incident)
- Number of shots: "shot five times" is the account in The Source, XXL, CBS and others; Rolling Stone Brasil states that The New York Times reported on Nov. 30, 1994 that Shakur took five shots during an alleged armed robbery ("levou cinco tiros durante um suposto assalto à mão armada") — [Rolling Stone Brasil](https://rollingstone.com.br/?p=107516); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/possible-tupac-shakur-shooter-in-94-robbery-comes-forward)
- Wound locations (versions differ): (a) "shot five times — in the head, groin, and left hand" after he "lunged at one of the gunmen in anger" — [Substack: Quad Recording Studios Incident](https://shamarieknight.substack.com/p/the-quad-recording-studios-incident); (b) "shot twice in the head, twice in the groin and once in one hand" — [Crime Library](https://www.crimelibrary.org/notorious_murders/celebrity/shakur_BIG/2a.html); (c) Fox 5 NY, cited by The Source, said one bullet "passed very close to his head" — [The Source 2025](https://thesource.com/2025/11/30/today-in-hip-hop-history-tupac-shakur-shot-and-robbed-in-quad-studios-31-years-ago/). A disputed low-quality page claimed "skull and crotch"; established outlets do not use that phrasing.
- Robbery: three armed Black men ambushed the group and stole jewelry worth about $40,000; one account says the robbers did not take his Rolex — [The Source 2019](https://thesource.com/2019/11/30/today-in-hip-hop-history-tupac-shakur-shot-5-times-at-quad-studios/); [Crime Library](https://www.crimelibrary.org/notorious_murders/celebrity/shakur_BIG/2a.html)
- Hospital and self-discharge: he was rushed to Bellevue Hospital for emergency surgery and "checked himself out of the hospital less than 3 hours after surgery" (another version: the next morning, three hours post-op); Bellevue doctors said that by leaving he risked his health, "possibly losing a testicle"; one account attributes the discharge to paranoia about being attacked again — [XXL](https://www.xxlmag.com/today-in-hip-hop-2pac-shot-at-quad-studios-in-new-york-city/); [Crime Library](https://www.crimelibrary.org/notorious_murders/celebrity/shakur_BIG/2a.html); [Dear Mama (2023) clip transcript](https://clip.cafe/dear-mama-2023/and-i-got-there-the-security-guard-like/)
- Convalescence: after discharge he recovered at actress Jasmine Guy's apartment — [Crime Library](https://www.crimelibrary.org/notorious_murders/celebrity/shakur_BIG/2a.html); the FX docuseries *Dear Mama* (2023) covers this recovery rather than the shooting itself — [Hollywood Reporter review](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-reviews/dear-mama-review-allen-hughes-tupac-afeni-shakur-1235392530/)
- Courtroom the next day: "the following day, he appeared at a Manhattan courthouse in a wheelchair and bandaged to receive the jury's verdict in his sexual abuse case"; the shooting is widely described as happening "the day before the verdict ... was due to be announced" — [Substack: Quad Recording Studios Incident](https://shamarieknight.substack.com/p/the-quad-recording-studios-incident); [The Source 2019](https://thesource.com/2019/11/30/today-in-hip-hop-history-tupac-shakur-shot-5-times-at-quad-studios/); a widely circulated photo of him in the wheelchair is discussed at [The Standard (Kenya)](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/entertainment/news/article/2001347578/iconic-photo-of-tupac-shakur-captured-after-he-was-shot)
- Later accusations: in a Vibe interview with Kevin Powell, Tupac "called out Sean 'Puffy' Combs and Biggie Smalls and accused both of them of setting him up, or of having knowledge of the attack and not warning him"; Vibe masked two associates, referring to James "Jimmy Henchman" Rosemond as "Booker" and Jacques "Haitian Jack" Agnant as "Nigel" — [Wikipedia, "Hit 'Em Up"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hit_%27Em_Up); [The Daily Beast](https://thedailybeast.com/tupacs-hit-em-up-the-most-savage-diss-track-ever-turns-20). Sources disagree whether this was the 1995 or 1996 Vibe interview.
- "Who Shot Ya?": Tupac interpreted Biggie's single as mockery of the Quad shooting and later said "Hit 'Em Up" (1996) was his response — [Wikipedia, "Hit 'Em Up"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hit_%27Em_Up)
- Unresolved responsibility: a March 2008 Los Angeles Times (Chuck Philips) report alleging Combs and Rosemond orchestrated the attack was denied by both and later discredited for relying on forged FBI documents from a con man — [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/report-con-man-at-center-of-la-times-1994-tupac-shooting-story-84686/); [Philadelphia Inquirer 2008](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/entertainment/20080318_Paper__Diddy__Rosemond_plotted_Shakur_shooting.html); in June 2011 convicted felon Dexter Isaac claimed on AllHipHop.com that Rosemond paid him to rob Tupac — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/possible-tupac-shakur-shooter-in-94-robbery-comes-forward); [CS Monitor](https://www.csmonitor.com/USA/Latest-News-Wires/2011/0616/Man-claims-responsibility-for-1994-Tupac-Shakur-shooting); [Okayplayer](https://www.okayplayer.com/news/tupac-dexter-isaac-jimmy-henchman-quad-studio-shooting.html). No one was ever charged.
- Vibe cover story: Kevin Powell's 1995 Vibe piece "Ready to Live" included an interview conducted at Rikers Island in which "Pac, in a nervous, harrowing interview ... recounted the Quad Studios shooting" — [Grammy.com, All Eyez on Me 25th anniversary](https://www.grammy.com/news/2021-tupac-all-eyez-on-me-25th-anniversary); [Wikipedia, Kevin Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Powell)

### Inferences
- The heart-monitor/hospital imagery at the opening of "Only God Can Judge Me" (recorded ~11 months after Quad) maps directly onto the documented sequence: surgery at Bellevue, lying wounded in intensive care, and leaving early out of fear for his life. The lyric about a doctor standing over him and a body full of bullet holes (paraphrased; see songmeaningsandfacts link in Q5) is consistent with the five-wound account.
- The "stabbed in the back"/betrayal motif in the song aligns with his stated belief, voiced to Vibe, that people in his own circle (Henchman, Haitian Jack, and by extension Biggie/Puffy) set him up — i.e., the betrayal was perceived as coming from inside hip-hop, not from police.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the original NYT (Dec. 1, 1994) or Daily News wire reports to confirm whether any contemporary report said "shot twice"; the only snippet-level evidence (Rolling Stone Brasil) says the NYT reported five shots. The "2–5 shots" discrepancy mentioned in the assignment is therefore unverified here.
- No source retrieved specifies the exact surgery performed or the duration of hospitalization in hours beyond "less than 3 hours after surgery."
- The exact Vibe issue (April 1995 vs. February 1996) in which Puffy/Biggie were named could not be pinned down from snippets.

## Key Question 2: The sexual-abuse trial, conviction (Dec. 1, 1994), sentencing (Feb. 1995), and the prison term (Rikers → Clinton Correctional, Feb.–Oct. 12, 1995)

### Takeaway
A Manhattan jury convicted Tupac in early December 1994 (after three days of deliberation) on sexual-abuse counts stemming from a November 1993 hotel-room incident, acquitting him of sodomy and weapons charges; Justice Daniel P. Fitzgerald sentenced him on Feb. 7 or 8, 1995 (sources split) to 18 months–4½ years, condemning the act as "brutal violence." He was held at Rikers Island, then transferred to Clinton Correctional Facility in Dannemora on March 8, 1995, where he married Keisha Morris (April 1995), read Machiavelli, gave the "Thug Life is dead" interview, and was released on $1.4 million bail (Suge Knight) on October 12, 1995 pending appeal — roughly eight to nine months after sentencing, eleven months after the verdict.

### Cited Findings
- Arrest: Tupac was arrested in New York on November 18, 1993 on sexual assault allegations — [The Source](https://thesource.com/2020/11/18/today-in-hip-hop-history-tupac-shakur-arrested-in-nyc-on-alleged-sexual-assault-charge-27-years-ago/); the accuser was later publicly identified as Ayanna Jackson — [Oxygen](https://www.oxygen.com/index%2Ephp/crime-time/tupac-rape-accuser-speaks-first-time-recalls-1993-assault); [Defender Network](https://defendernetwork.com/lead-story/woman-accused-tupac-rape-1993-speaks-first-time/)
- Verdict: the jury convicted on sexual-abuse counts and acquitted on sodomy and firearm charges after three days of deliberation; one count breakdown says guilty on three counts of sexual abuse, acquitted on two counts of sodomy, one count of attempted sodomy and three counts of criminal weapons possession — [Encyclopedia.com, "Tupac Shakur Trial: 1994-95"](https://www.encyclopedia.com/law/law-magazines/tupac-shakur-trial-1994-95); [XXL](https://www.xxlmag.com/tupac-sexual-assault-case-sentence/). Snippets place the verdict in "December 1994"; the Dec. 1 date is supported indirectly by the "day after the shooting" accounts in Q1.
- Judge and sentence: Manhattan State Supreme Court Justice Daniel P. Fitzgerald imposed 18 months to 4½ years; he "rejected the defense's characterization of the event and condemned the act as 'brutal violence'" — [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/law/law-magazines/tupac-shakur-trial-1994-95); [RareNewspapers listing of Feb. 1995 paper](https://www.rarenewspapers.com/newspaper/728706-tupac-shakur-sentenced-in-new-yori-city)
- Sentencing date disputed: XXL and Complex give February 7, 1995 — [Complex, "This Day in Rap History: 2Pac Sentenced Feb. 7"](https://www.complex.com/music/a/elva-aguilar/this-day-in-rap-history-2pac-sentenced-feb-7); [XXL](https://www.xxlmag.com/tupac-sexual-assault-case-sentence/); Encyclopedia.com and a Los Angeles Times page dated Feb. 8, 1995 give February 8 — [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/law/law-magazines/tupac-shakur-trial-1994-95)
- Courtroom statement: Tupac "maintained his innocence throughout the trial, addressing the court prior to sentencing to refuse an apology and assert that he had been betrayed by the judicial system"; Shock G discussed the speech in *Thug Angel* (2002) — [RareNewspapers](https://www.rarenewspapers.com/newspaper/728706-tupac-shakur-sentenced-in-new-yori-city); [Distractify](https://www.distractify.com/p/why-did-tupac-shakur-go-to-jail)
- Rikers then Clinton: he was first held at Rikers Island (where Powell interviewed him in January 1995, per one account) — [Rap Radar Substack](https://rapradarpodcast.substack.com/p/2pacs-most-iconic-interviews-vol); [Newsweek, Kevin Powell, "The Tupac Shakur I Knew"](https://www.newsweek.com/the-tupac-shakur-i-knew-12429890); "On March 8th, 1995, 2Pac Shakur ... arrived at the Clinton Correctional facility in Dannemora, New York"; "His seven-month confinement culminated in an unexpected bail release on October 12th" — [OverDrive listing, *Tupac Behind Bars*](https://company.cdn.overdrive.com/media/9715112/tupac-behind-bars); [Microcosm Publishing, *Tupac Behind Bars*](https://microcosmpublishing.com/catalog/books/42579)
- Total time served: sources vary — "about nine months" ([Distractify](https://www.distractify.com/p/why-did-tupac-shakur-go-to-jail)), "eight months" and "eleven and a half months" (various, via [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/2021-tupac-all-eyez-on-me-25th-anniversary) and NPR coverage). Reconciliation: ~11 months from the Dec. 1994 verdict (he was remanded after the verdict), ~8 months from the Feb. 1995 sentencing, ~7 months at Clinton.
- *Me Against the World* No. 1 while incarcerated: released March 14, 1995; debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200 the week of April 1, 1995 and held for four weeks; widely described as the first No. 1 album by an artist in prison — [Wikipedia, Me Against the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me_Against_the_World); [number1albums.com](https://number1albums.com/2pac-me-against-the-world-april-1-1995/); [Spin](https://www.spinmagazine.com/2020/03/tupac-me-against-the-world-25th-anniversary/)
- Marriage: Tupac married Keisha Morris in a ceremony at Clinton Correctional Facility; most sources give April 29, 1995 (two say April 4); the marriage was annulled (some say divorced) about ten months later, c. March 1996 — [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/ask-legit/biographies/1481117-keisha-morris-biography-tupacs-wife-today/); [Yen.com.gh](https://yen.com.gh/facts-lifehacks/biographies/208493-meet-keisha-morris-tupacs-wife-where-today/); a June 1997 *Sister 2 Sister* interview with Morris is referenced in aggregator pages. - Prison interview (Clinton, 1995): footage was recorded at Clinton Correctional Facility in 1995 and later released as *Tupac: Uncensored and Uncut: The Lost Prison Tapes* (dir. Ken Peters) — [BET](https://www.bet.com/article/g2etuu/tupac-opens-up-in-1995-prison-interview); [Fountaindale Library record](https://link.fountaindale.org/resource/t_cRbiSMHJY). A 1995 line attributed to this prison period: "It's a struggle for every young Black man. You know how it is, only God can judge us" — [HotNewHipHop](https://www.hotnewhiphop.com/1007487-tupac-resurfaced-1995-interview)
- Bail and release: Knight "posted his $1.4 million bail and freed him from prison pending an appeal of his conviction, while signing him to Death Row Records" — [Wikipedia, Suge Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suge_Knight); released October 12, 1995 — [OverDrive, *Tupac Behind Bars*](https://company.cdn.overdrive.com/media/9715112/tupac-behind-bars). Conflicting amount: former Interscope A&R Leslie Gerard told Los Angeles Magazine Knight "offered to pay his $3 million bail on the condition he sign with Knight's Death Row Records" — [Los Angeles Magazine, "Tupac Changes / Suge Knight"](https://www.lamag.com/culturefiles/tupac-changes-suge-knight/)
- Civil suits during this period: the wrongful-death suit over 6-year-old Qa'id Walker-Teal (killed by a stray bullet during a 1992 Marin City festival altercation involving Tupac's entourage) was settled in November 1995 for an undisclosed amount after two witnesses testified — [Deseret News, Nov. 13, 1995](https://www.deseret.com/1995/11/13/19204429/wrongful-death-suit-against-shakur-is-settled/); [Sausalito Historical Society](https://www.sausalitohistoricalsociety.com/2023-columns/2023/9/13/rappers-roots-in-marin-city)

### Inferences
- "Only God Can Judge Me" was written by a man who had just spent ~11 months imprisoned on a conviction he publicly refused to accept, after a judge had explicitly "judged" the act as brutal violence; the title can be read as a direct rejoinder to that courtroom judgment and to the press verdicts that accompanied it.
- The prison months were also when he explicitly began constructing the Makaveli persona (Aug. 1995 letter) — the song therefore sits at the hinge between the "Thug Life is dead" repentance mode of early 1995 and the fatalistic, embattled Makaveli mode of 1996.

### Gaps
- Exact verdict date (Dec. 1) is inferred from "day after the shooting" accounts; no snippet gave the explicit date.
- No verbatim text of Tupac's pre-sentencing statement or of Fitzgerald's full remarks was retrievable.
- No primary-source confirmation of the Morris wedding date or whether the marriage ended by annulment or divorce.
- The Atlanta (Oct. 31, 1993) off-duty police shooting case and the Allen Hughes assault conviction (1994) were not surfaced by searches and are not documented here.

## Key Question 3: The Vibe prison interview ("Thug Life is dead"), the Death Row contract, and the All Eyez on Me sessions at Can-Am

### Takeaway
In Kevin Powell's 1995 Vibe cover story "Ready to Live" (interview at Rikers, early 1995), Tupac declared "Thug Life to me is dead," denounced the persona and accepted responsibility for his music's impact; by the time Knight bailed him out (Oct. 12, 1995) he had signed a three-page handwritten Death Row contract (dated Sept. 16, 1995, naming Knight as manager and David Kenner as lawyer), went straight from the airport to Can-Am Studios in Tarzana, and recorded the double album between Oct. 13 and Dec. 18, 1995 — with multiple participants describing the bulk of it being done in about two weeks. He told MTV in December 1995 the title reflected that police, the Feds, enemies and friends were all watching him; the album was released February 13, 1996.

### Cited Findings
- "Ready to Live": Powell's 1995 Vibe cover story "featuring an interview conducted while Shakur was jailed in Rikers Island ... became one of the magazine's bestsellers" — [Wikipedia, Kevin Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Powell); Powell met Tupac at Rikers in January 1995 per [Rap Radar Substack](https://rapradarpodcast.substack.com/p/2pacs-most-iconic-interviews-vol); Powell described the setting: "at the Rikers Island prison complex, with his publicist, lawyer, a photographer, and prison guards lurking nearby" — [Newsweek, "The Tupac Shakur I Knew"](https://www.newsweek.com/the-tupac-shakur-i-knew-12429890)
- "Thug Life is dead": the Tampa Bay Times (Sept. 15, 1996) rendered the quote as "Thug Life to me is dead" — [Tampa Bay Times, "Gangsta rapper Tupac Shakur lived, died in conflict"](https://www.tampabay.com/archive/1996/09/15/gangsta-rapper-tupac-shakur-lived-died-in-conflict/); Grammy.com: he "denounced the thug life persona he had adopted and assumed responsibility for his music's impact" — [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/2021-tupac-all-eyez-on-me-25th-anniversary); a later quote in which he reverses himself: "Yes, I did say Thug Life was dead, but when [New York's hip-hop scene] said [they didn't have information on my shooting], they breathed new life into me" — [Rap Radar Substack](https://rapradarpodcast.substack.com/p/2pacs-most-iconic-interviews-vol) (wording not confirmed against Vibe)
- "No fear of death": "In a now famous Vibe magazine interview the year before his death at the age of 25, Shakur told journalist Kevin Powell, 'I don't have no fear of death'"; a quote site extends it "My only fear is coming back reincarnated" but attributes it elsewhere — [CTV News, "Tupac often talked about dying young"](https://www.ctvnews.ca/entertainment/article/tupac-often-talked-about-dying-young-thats-part-of-why-his-murder-still-haunts-us/); [This Is Africa](https://thisisafrica.me/arts-and-culture/remembering-rap-legend-tupac-shakur-10-quotes/)
- Powell on Tupac's sense of time: Tupac "wanted him to be a kind of biographer, comparing him to Alex Haley, because he knew from the very beginning he had a short window to create work" — [NPR, "Tupac Shakur's Legacy, 20 Years On" (Sept. 13, 2016)](https://www.npr.org/2016/09/13/493671606/tupac-shakurs-legacy-20-years-on)
- Contract paperwork: a July 30, 1995 statement signed by Tupac and witnessed gave David Kenner "permission to negotiate on my behalf"; Tupac "signed a three-page handwritten contract with Suge Knight on September 16, 1995," negotiations having begun about two months earlier; it named Knight as manager and Kenner as lawyer, a dual role Afeni later criticized as a conflict of interest — [Bonhams, lot 1341, Tupac handwritten letter](https://www.bonhams.com/auction/21818/lot/1341/a-tupac-shakur-handwritten-letter); [Bonhams lot 1342](https://www.bonhams.com/auctions/21818/lot/1342); [XXL, "Tupac letters"](https://www.xxlmag.com/tupac-letters-oukast-west-coast-east-coast/)
- Competing "napkin" account: Staci Robinson's authorized biography (2023) says the $3.5 million Death Row contract "was signed on a napkin" and "drawn up in the visiting room of the prison" — [Yahoo News, "5 things you might not know about Tupac Shakur, from a new authorized biography"](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/5-things-might-not-know-134200642.html). A former corrections officer reportedly said Tupac compared the Death Row contract to a "devil's deal" in 1995 — [Music Times (Dec. 2025)](https://www.musictimes.com/articles/110558/20251201/excorrections-officer-reveals-tupac-compared-death-row-contract-devils-deal-1995.htm)
- Deal terms: the album "was released following an agreement between Knight and Shakur that called for three albums under Death Row in return for bail payment" — [Wikipedia, All Eyez on Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Eyez_on_Me); Knight "went to Interscope president Jimmy Iovine and asked for the contract to be transferred" (Leslie Gerard) — [Los Angeles Magazine](https://www.lamag.com/culturefiles/tupac-changes-suge-knight/)
- Straight to the studio: "when Suge got him out, Pac went straight into the studio and wrote All Eyez on Me" (Alex Roberts) — [Los Angeles Magazine](https://www.lamag.com/culturefiles/tupac-changes-suge-knight/); the LA Times found him "working at a rapid pace almost immediately after his release, with more than a dozen songs already recorded by the time the newspaper interviewed him that October" — [HotNewHipHop](https://www.hotnewhiphop.com/1007487-tupac-resurfaced-1995-interview); the Oct. 1995 LA Times piece by Chuck Philips is listed as "Tupac Shakur: 'I Am Not a Gangster'" — [Shmoop bibliography](https://shmoop.com/study-guides/2pac-dear-mama/websites.html)
- Session dates/location: recorded October 13 – December 18, 1995 at Can-Am Studios, Tarzana, Los Angeles — [Wikipedia, All Eyez on Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Eyez_on_Me); Can-Am was "a place that Death Row had leased for like two years" (engineer quoted) — [Los Angeles Magazine](https://www.lamag.com/culturefiles/tupac-changes-suge-knight/)

- Title and paranoia: the album was originally to be called *Euthanasia*; Tupac told MTV's Bill Bellamy in December 1995 that he felt watched by law enforcement and listed hostile ex-partners, jealous friends and loyal associates among those paying attention, ending that everyone wants to see what he will do next; songs such as "Can't C Me" allude to being watched — [Wikipedia, All Eyez on Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Eyez_on_Me); a 25th-anniversary retrospective: "the paranoia, and the belief that everyone was anticipating his next move, imbued All Eyez On Me with a potent energy" and "death haunted him everywhere he went" — [Albumism, 25th anniversary retrospective](https://albumism.com/features/2pac-all-eyez-on-me-turns-25-anniversary-retrospective)
- MTV Tabitha Soren interview: conducted October 27, 1995 on Venice Beach, "just two weeks after he was released from prison"; the full 20-minute version was released by MTV in 2019 — [BET](https://www.bet.com/music/2019/09/16/tupac-mtv-1995-interview.html); [Okayplayer](https://www.okayplayer.com/video/tupac-mtv-venice-beach-interview-tabitha-soren.html)
- Release and reception: *All Eyez on Me* released February 13, 1996 — [SiriusXM](https://www.siriusxm.com/blog/2pac-all-eyez-on-me); review aggregation (via Wikipedia table): Pitchfork 9.4/10, NME 9/10, Spin 7/10; The Source 5 mics; Rolling Stone 3/5 in 1996 and 5/5 in the 2004 album guide; AllMusic (Steve Huey) 5/5; Rolling Stone placed the album No. 436 on its 2020 "500 Greatest Albums" list — [Wikipedia, All Eyez on Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Eyez_on_Me); [BestEverAlbums compiled ratings](https://www.besteveralbums.com/thechart.php?a=1142); [Nicolle Okoren Substack, "#436: 2Pac, All Eyez on Me"](https://nicolleokoren.substack.com/p/436-2pac-all-eyez-on-me). The Guardian's 25th-anniversary piece called it "the Sgt Pepper's of hip-hop" — [Guardian via Yahoo](https://news.yahoo.com/sgt-pepper-hip-hop-2pac-063000444.html)
- Vibe "Live From Death Row" cover (dated Feb. 1996 by Andscape; Dec. 1995/Jan. 1996 by Complex) featured Tupac, Snoop Dogg, Dr. Dre and Suge Knight in a *Goodfellas*-inspired shoot; it was Powell's third and last Tupac cover before the murder — [Andscape](https://andscape.com/features/all-eyez-on-vibe-magazines-1996-death-row-cover); [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/rob-kenner/vibe-1996-east-coast-vs-west-coast-cover); [Wikipedia, Kevin Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Powell)

### Inferences
- The chronology (contract Sept. 16 → release Oct. 12 → first Can-Am session Oct. 13) means "Only God Can Judge Me" was almost certainly cut within the first weeks of freedom, when the hospital and courtroom memories were months old and the Death Row obligation (three albums for bail) was brand new.
- The pivot from "Thug Life is dead" (Jan. 1995) to the defiant, surveillance-haunted album (Oct.–Dec. 1995) is well documented and the song's title encapsulates it: he no longer seeks the public's absolution, only God's.

## Key Question 4: Tupac's stated religious/spiritual worldview — God, church, "Black Jesus," heaven, judgment

### Takeaway
Tupac, raised by Black Panther Afeni Shakur (who herself moved through Islam and Yoruba study), professed belief in God and heaven while rejecting institutional churches as corporate entities that failed the poor; his recurring figure of a "Black Jesus"/"Black Jesuz" — a God who "must look like the people the world throws away" — is read by Michael Eric Dyson and others as a "thug theodicy": a sustained interrogation of suffering, evil and divine judgment rather than orthodox piety. The phrase "only God can judge us" appears in his 1995 prison-era speech before it became a song title.

### Cited Findings
- Afeni's path: she joined the Black Panthers around age 21, converted to Islam after meeting Lumumba Shakur, and later "studied Yoruba culture and religion" with a teacher who gave her a new name — [Biography.com, Afeni Shakur](https://www.biography.com/personality/afeni-shakur); [USC Scalar, Legacy of Black Panther Sisters](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/the-legacy-of-black-panther-sisters/afeni-shakur); the Panther 21 used limited bail money to free her before trial — [Hollywood Reporter, Dear Mama review](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-reviews/dear-mama-review-allen-hughes-tupac-afeni-shakur-1235392530/)
- Karma/heaven: in a widely circulated interview he said he believed "your karma, everything that you do bad, comes back to you. So anything that I'm doing that's bad, I'm gonna have to suffer for, but in my heart, I think what I'm doing is right," which led him to feel he was going to heaven — [This Is Africa, 10 quotes](https://thisisafrica.me/arts-and-culture/remembering-rap-legend-tupac-shakur-10-quotes/) (date of clip not given)
- "Only God can judge us" (1995): "It's a struggle for every young Black man. You know how it is, only God can judge us" — [HotNewHipHop, resurfaced 1995 interview](https://www.hotnewhiphop.com/1007487-tupac-resurfaced-1995-interview)
- Churches: in a 1996 Vibe interview he criticized churches for not returning money to the community, saying if churches gave back half their money "we'd be a'ight" — [Zócalo Public Square / ASU, "Tupac Was an Imperfect Prophet"](https://live-zocalopublicsquare.ws.asu.edu/?p=140175); a commentary says he "viewed the church as a corporate entity that had failed the poor, yet he couldn't shake the presence of God" — [Presbyterian Outlook, "Tupac Shakur: poetic, prophetic, profound" (Nov. 2025)](https://pres-outlook.org/2025/11/tupac-shakur-poetic-prophetic-profound/)
- "God must look like the people the world throws away": the same Presbyterian Outlook episode describes him as a "street theologian" and lists "Hail Mary" and "spiritual warfare in a world obsessed with death" among apocalyptic themes — [Presbyterian Outlook](https://pres-outlook.org/2025/11/tupac-shakur-poetic-prophetic-profound/)
- Dyson's "thug theodicy": in *Holler If You Hear Me* (2001) Dyson argues Tupac's contribution was "his ability to ask hard questions about suffering and evil," calls him a "hip-hop Jeremiah," and reads "Black Jesus" as "a railing response and prophetic critique against the dogmatism that Tupac experienced in religious communities" — [The American Prospect review](https://prospect.org/2002/02/05/tupac-world/); [The Stranger review](https://www.thestranger.com/?p=20225); [Dustin Zielke, *Illumine* (UVic)](https://journals.uvic.ca/index.php/Illumine/article/view/10733/2909); [Georgetown Berkley Center, Dyson on hip-hop theology](https://berkleycenter.georgetown.edu/posts/michael-eric-dyson-on-hip-hop-theology)
- Obsession with death and God: Dyson "shows how Tupac was obsessed with death and the idea of God in a chaotic thug world," likened to the Old Testament, "entrenched in sex, violence, and confused moralism" — [ThriftBooks summary of Holler If You Hear Me](https://www.thriftbooks.com/w/holler-if-you-hear-me_michael-eric-dyson/328806/item/)
- Academic readings: John Freeman, "Tupac's Quest for Black Jesus: God as Deadbeat Dad and Afeni, the Migdala," *Journal of Interdisciplinary Feminist Thought* 9 (2016) reads his relationship with God through his absent father — [Salve Regina Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.salve.edu/jift/vol9/iss1/3); Elijah Gipson-Davis, "Tupac: a Black Christ?" (Harvard Divinity School, 2025) reads *The Don Killuminati* through the Black Social Gospel and Kelly Brown Douglas's Black Christ — [HDS](https://pes.hds.harvard.edu/publication/tupac-black-christ); Jenny Portillo's Fordham thesis "Thugz Theology" places him alongside DMX and Nas — [Fordham](https://research.library.fordham.edu/aaas_senior/27); Nomos Journal, "No Church in the Wild: ... Tupac's 'Black Jesuz'" — [Nomos Journal](https://nomosjournal.org/2013/03/no-church-in-the-wild/)
- One account says he described "feeling cursed by God" in a 1994 Ed Gordon (BET) interview — [Shortform, "Tupac faith"](https://shortform.com/books/blog/tupac-faith.html) (secondary; not verified)
- Posthumous use of the phrase: Jeff Pearlman's 2025 biography is titled *Only God Can Judge Me: The Many Lives of Tupac Shakur* (HarperCollins/Mariner; ~650–700 interviews over three years) — [Jeff Pearlman site](https://jeffpearlman.com/only-god-can-judge-me/); [Awful Announcing interview](https://awfulannouncing.com/books/jeff-pearlman-new-tupac-shakur-book-only-god-can-judge-me.html); [WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/2025-10-23/only-god-can-judge-me-explores-the-life-of-rapper-tupac-shakur); [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/10/21/arts/tupac-shakur-book); America Magazine calls the phrase "one of Tupac's most popular songs ... and the title of a recent book" — [America Magazine](https://www.americamagazine.org/?p=252650)

### Inferences
- The song's theology is consistent with the documented pattern: direct appeals to God, explicit distrust of human (judicial, media, church) authority, and a conviction that his own heart is right even if his actions are judged wrong — the "karma/heaven" quote is essentially the song's thesis in prose.
- The lineage runs: "So Many Tears" and "Lord Knows" (*Me Against the World*, 1995, written pre-prison) → "Only God Can Judge Me" (late 1995) → "Hail Mary" and "Blasphemy" (*Don Killuminati*, 1996) → "Black Jesuz" (Outlawz, *Still I Rise*, 1999) → "Ghetto Gospel" (posthumous, *Loyal to the Game*, 2004). (Song-title chronology is common knowledge not sourced in snippets; see Gaps.)

### Gaps
- I found no reliable primary source confirming that Tupac had an "Only God Can Judge Me" tattoo; tattoo pages surfaced were spam/SEO. His known religious tattoos (e.g., the "EXODUS 18:11" cross on his back) also could not be verified from a reputable source in this session. Do not assert the tattoo.
- No verbatim "Black Jesus" explanation from Tupac himself was retrievable (only Dyson's and others' readings).
- Lyric-level analysis of "So Many Tears," "Hail Mary," "Ghetto Gospel," and "Black Jesuz" was not found in retrievable sources.

## Key Question 5: How the song's paranoia, persecution and defiance fit his 1995–96 state of mind, Death Row, the East–West feud, political attacks, and his death

### Takeaway
The song's imagery of surveillance, betrayal and imminent death reflects a documented 1995–96 condition: Tupac told MTV in December 1995 that police, the Feds, enemies and friends were all watching him; he carried on a public war with Bad Boy ("Hit 'Em Up," June 1996); he was a named target of Dan Quayle (1992), Bob Dole/William Bennett/C. Delores Tucker (1995), and attacked Tucker by name on *All Eyez on Me*, drawing a $10 million suit; he adopted the Makaveli persona, recorded *The Don Killuminati* in days in August 1996, and was shot in Las Vegas on September 7, 1996, dying September 13 at age 25.

### Cited Findings
- December 1995 MTV explanation of the title (Bill Bellamy interview): he felt watched by law enforcement and by hostile ex-partners, jealous friends and loyal associates, "everyone wants to see what he will do next" — [Wikipedia, All Eyez on Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Eyez_on_Me); Albumism quotes him saying he had police and the Feds watching him — [Albumism 25th anniversary](https://albumism.com/features/2pac-all-eyez-on-me-turns-25-anniversary-retrospective)
- 1996 paranoia on record: an unreleased 1996 Vibe interview (conducted about two weeks before his death) is described as "erratic & paranoid" — [Okayplayer](https://www.okayplayer.com/read-tupacs-erratic-paranoid-unreleased-1996-interview/635643); a 1994 Entertainment Weekly interview (Benjamin Svetkey) is described as one in which he "forecasts his own untimely demise" — [Biography.com](https://www.biography.com/musicians/tupac-final-days-death); "Tupac often talked about dying young" — [CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/entertainment/article/tupac-often-talked-about-dying-young-thats-part-of-why-his-murder-still-haunts-us/)
- Dan Quayle (Sept. 1992): after a Texas trooper's killing, Vice President Quayle called on Interscope to pull *2Pacalypse Now*: "There is absolutely no reason for a record like this to be published ... it has no place in our society"; Interscope/Time Warner refused — [Wikipedia, 2Pacalypse Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2Pacalypse_Now); [Westword](https://www.westword.com/?p=40614532)
- Dole/Bennett/Tucker (1995): C. DeLores Tucker (National Political Congress of Black Women) and William Bennett launched a public anti-rap campaign in May 1995; "Succumbing to months of pressure from Senate Majority Leader Bob Dole and media watchdogs, Time Warner Inc. decided yesterday to get out of the gangsta rap business" (Sept. 27, 1995), i.e., selling its Interscope stake; Dole: "shame is a powerful weapon - and Time Warner has felt the sting of shame" — [Seattle Times, Sept. 28, 1995](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/19950928/2143958/time-warner-ditches-gangsta-rap----dole-media-watchdogs-credit-their-attacks); Death Row sued Tucker in August 1995 — [UPI, Aug. 15, 1995](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1995/08/15/Record-label-sues-rap-opponent/3415808459200/)
- Tucker lawsuit (1997): Tucker sued Shakur's estate and record companies/distributors/retailers in federal court in Philadelphia in 1997 for $10 million over "How Do U Want It" and "Wonda Why They Call U Bytch" (both on *All Eyez on Me*), alleging emotional distress, slander and invasion of privacy; dismissed 1999, upheld on appeal (unpublished) in 2000 — [Deseret News, Aug. 2, 1997](https://www.deseret.com/1997/8/2/19326940/rap-foe-says-lyrics-ruined-sex-sues-for-10-million/); [Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press, Summer 2000](https://www.rcfp.org/journals/the-news-media-and-the-law-summer-2000/shakurs-lyrics-attacking-ga/); [Wikipedia, C. Delores Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Delores_Tucker); [Wikipedia, How Do U Want It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_Do_U_Want_It)
- East–West feud: "Hit 'Em Up" (1996) targeted Biggie and Puffy and was Tupac's stated response to "Who Shot Ya?" — [Wikipedia, Hit 'Em Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hit_%27Em_Up); [Daily Beast](https://thedailybeast.com/tupacs-hit-em-up-the-most-savage-diss-track-ever-turns-20); the Vibe "East Coast vs. West Coast" cover framed the feud nationally — [Complex, Vibe 1996 cover](https://www.complex.com/music/a/rob-kenner/vibe-1996-east-coast-vs-west-coast-cover)
- Makaveli: *The Don Killuminati: The 7 Day Theory*, recorded at Can-Am in August 1996 (accounts: three, seven or twelve days), released under the name Makaveli on November 5, 1996 and debuted at No. 1 — [BET oral history](https://www.bet.com/article/iwy0u5/the-oral-history-of-2pacs-makaveli-album-how-a-michael-jackson-feature-almost-happened); [Las Vegas Sun, Nov. 15, 1996](https://lasvegassun.com/news/1996/nov/15/final-2pac-cd-hits-chart-at-no-1/); [Wikipedia, The Don Killuminati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Don_Killuminati:_The_7_Day_Theory)
- Death: shot about 11:15 p.m. on September 7, 1996 in a drive-by at Flamingo Road and Koval Lane, Las Vegas, after attending the Mike Tyson–Bruce Seldon fight at the MGM Grand with Suge Knight (who was grazed); a white/cream Cadillac pulled alongside Knight's black BMW; Tupac was hit four times; taken to University Medical Center, where a lung was removed and he was kept on a respirator; died September 13, 1996, pronounced at 4:03 p.m., age 25 — [The Source](https://thesource.com/2026/09/07/today-in-hip-hop-history-tupac-shakur-was-shot-on-the-las-vegas-strip-30-years-ago/); [History.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/september-13/tupac-shakur-dies); [TheGrio, "Inside the night Tupac was shot"](https://thegrio.com/2023/09/30/inside-the-night-tupac-died/); [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/the-world-remembers-tupac-today-25-years-after-his-death/)
- Case status: Duane "Keffe D" Davis was arrested in September 2023 and charged with murder (prosecutors did not allege he pulled the trigger); he was convicted in 2026 and sentenced in October 2026 to 40 years to life — [TheGrio, Oct. 13, 2026](https://thegrio.com/2026/10/13/duane-davis-sentenced-40-years-life-tupac-shakur/); [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/duane-davis-sentenced-to-40-years-in-prison-for-murder-of-tupac-shakur/); [NY1/AP, forensic testimony Aug. 18, 2026](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/ap-top-news/2026/08/18/forensic-pathologist-details-tupac-shakurs-fatal-wounds-at-duane-keffe-d-davis-trial)
- Rolling Stone's posthumous cover: issue 746, dated October 31, 1996, with a Danny Clinch portrait originally shot in 1993 — [Rolling Stone, key covers of the 1990s](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/lists/100-greatest-artists-of-all-time-19691231/nirvana-19691231); [eBay listing, RS 746](https://www.ebay.com/itm/156955404955)
- Tampa Bay Times obituary framing (Sept. 15, 1996): "Gangsta rapper Tupac Shakur lived, died in conflict" — [Tampa Bay Times](https://www.tampabay.com/archive/1996/09/15/gangsta-rapper-tupac-shakur-lived-died-in-conflict/)

### Inferences
- The song's refusal of earthly judgment addresses, simultaneously, the New York court, the national politicians (Quayle, Dole, Tucker) who had made him a symbol, the press that covered his trial, and the hip-hop peers he believed had set him up. Its fatalism ("I'd rather die like a man than live like a coward" sentiment, paraphrased) was borne out within ten months of release.
- Death Row's business model (three albums for bail) and Knight's control over Can-Am sessions explain both the album's speed and the pressure that colleagues (Alex Roberts, engineers quoted in LA Mag) describe; the song's "stress" and "paranoia" lines are contemporaneous with that pressure.

## Key Question 6: The song's place on *All Eyez on Me* (Book 1, track 10), relation to neighboring tracks, credits, and critical/cultural legacy

### Takeaway
"Only God Can Judge Me" (feat. Rappin' 4-Tay; produced by Doug Rasheed and Harold "Scrap" Freddie) sits at track 10 of Book 1, between Johnny J's "Life Goes On" (track 9, a eulogy for dead friends) and "Tradin War Stories" (track 11), following DJ Quik's "Heartz of Men" (track 8) — a run that forms the album's mortality core. It was never a single and does not appear in major-outlet top-10 lists that I could verify, but it is a fixture of fan and mid-tier critical rankings, was placed on *The Hate U Give* (2018) soundtrack, and lent its title to Jeff Pearlman's 2025 biography; its hospital/heart-monitor opening is widely read as a dramatization of the Quad shooting aftermath.
