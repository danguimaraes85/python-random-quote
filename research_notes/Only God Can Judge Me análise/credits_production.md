## Key Question 1 — Who is credited as writer and producer (liner notes, Discogs, AllMusic, ASCAP/BMI, Genius), and where do sources disagree?

### Takeaway
All sources agree the track is produced by **Doug Rasheed** and **Harold "Scrap" Freddie**; writer credits are inconsistent — Wikipedia, IMDb and Shazam list four writers (Tupac Shakur, Doug Rasheed, Anthony Forté, Harold Fretty/Freddie), while Deezer, WhoSampled and the 2Pac Fandom wiki list only Shakur and Rasheed. The surname of the second producer appears in at least three spellings: "Scrap Freddie" (producer credit), "Harold Fretty" / "Harold Scrap Fretty" / "Harold A. Fretty" (writer credit in Wikipedia, Shazam, licensing data).

### Cited Findings
- Wikipedia's *All Eyez on Me* track listing credits the writers of "Only God Can Judge Me" (featuring Rappin' 4-Tay) as Shakur, Doug Rasheed, Anthony Forté and **Harold Fretty**, and the producers as Doug Rasheed and **"Harold Scrap Freddie"**; the track is Book 1, track 10 — [Wikipedia: All Eyez on Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Eyez_on_Me)
- IMDb's soundtrack page for the 2017 biopic *All Eyez on Me* lists the same four writers (Shakur, Rasheed, Forté, Fretty) — [IMDb soundtrack](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1666185/soundtrack/)
- IMDb Pro's Doug Rasheed page shows him credited as writer of "Only God Can Judge Me" in *All Eyez on Me* (2017) and *The Hate U Give* (2018) — [IMDb Pro: Doug Rasheed](https://pro.imdb.com/name/nm10364055)
- Shazam lists songwriters as Tupac Shakur, Anthony Forté, Doug Rasheed and **"Harold Scrap Fretty"**, released February 13, 1996 by Amaru Entertainment, Inc./Interscope Records as part of *All Eyez On Me* — [Shazam](https://www.shazam.com/song/1588481163/only-god-can-judge-me-feat-rappin-4-tay)
- A licensing/copyright-holder record (EasySong) credits composers as "4 Tay, Harold Fretty, Douglas Rasheed, Tupak Shakur [sic]" with publishing through Universal Music and related entities — [EasySong copyright-holder lookup](https://www.easysong.com/search/songs/song-copyright-holder-information.aspx?s=1962228)
- Deezer credits writer/composer as "Tupac Shakur, Douglas Rasheed" only — [Deezer](https://www.deezer.com/us/track/1510293712)
- WhoSampled lists producers "Doug Rasheed, Harold Scrap Freddie," writers "2Pac, Doug Rasheed," and Rappin' 4-Tay as guest vocalist — [WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/2Pac/Only-God-Can-Judge-Me/)
- The 2Pac Fandom album page lists writers as Rasheed and Shakur only — [2pac.fandom.com](https://2pac.fandom.com/wiki/All_Eyez_on_Me_(album)); the Hip-Hop Music Fandom page also carries the album credits — [hip-hop-music.fandom.com](https://hip-hop-music.fandom.com/wiki/All_Eyez_on_Me)
- An older Wikipedia mirror (en-academic) of a since-deleted "Only God Can Judge Me (song)" article reportedly lists producers as "Doug Rasheed, Alcotraz, and Tyke Dawg" — this conflicts with every other source and should be treated as an error in that old article — [en-academic mirror](https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/5494021)
- A fan tracklisting page credits writers "2pac and D. Rasheed," producer Doug Rasheed, featuring Rappin' 4-Tay — [acatict.tripod.com](https://acatict.tripod.com/alleyezonme/ogcjm.html)
- Song Meanings and Facts summarises: "Tupac and Rappin' 4-Tay wrote this song with additional input from its producers, D. Rasheed and H.S. Freddie" — [songmeaningsandfacts.com](https://songmeaningsandfacts.com/only-god-can-judge-me-by-2pac-ft-rappin-4-tay/)
- Track length is given as 4:56 on Apple Music/Deezer/The Prophet compilation and 4:57 on *The Hate U Give* soundtrack listing — [Apple Music](https://music.apple.com/us/song/only-god-can-judge-me-feat-rappin-4-tay/1588481163); [Deezer (THUG soundtrack, 04:57)](https://www.deezer.com/en/track/564972842); [Wikipedia: The Prophet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prophet:_The_Best_of_the_Works)

### Inferences
- "Freddie" vs. "Fretty": the producer credit "Harold Scrap Freddie" (as reproduced from the liner notes by Wikipedia/WhoSampled) and the writer credit "Harold (A.) Fretty" (Shazam, EasySong, Wikipedia writer column) almost certainly refer to the same person; the PRO/publisher registration likely uses "Fretty" as the legal surname and the liner notes used the nickname-style "Scrap Freddie". The report should present both spellings and flag the discrepancy rather than pick one.
- The two-writer listings (Shakur/Rasheed) on streaming metadata are probably truncated metadata rather than a different registration; the four-writer version appears in the licensing record, which is the closer-to-primary source available here.
- No source distinguishes "producer" from "co-producer"; both Rasheed and Scrap Freddie are listed flatly as producers. Rasheed, however, is the only one who discusses the track publicly and is credited as sole writer in the two-name versions, suggesting he was the lead producer.

### Gaps
- Could not open Genius, Discogs, AllMusic, ASCAP ACE or BMI Songview, so the exact credits box / work ID / IPI / publisher splits are unverified. Multiple searches for BMI/ASCAP registration returned nothing.
- Physical liner-note scan not accessed; "as on the liner notes" spelling is inferred from Wikipedia/WhoSampled transcription only.

## Key Question 2 — Who are Doug Rasheed and Harold "Scrap" Freddie/Fretty? Other credits? Interviews?

### Takeaway
Doug Rasheed is a Grammy-nominated producer best known for Coolio's "Gangsta's Paradise" (1995); he also produced for Montell Jordan, Whitney Houston, Brandy, LL Cool J, Chaka Khan (whom he married in 2001), produced tracks on Rappin' 4-Tay's *Off Parole* (1996), published a 2026 memoir, and has given several interviews specifically about "Only God Can Judge Me." Harold "Scrap" Freddie/Fretty is essentially undocumented beyond this credit — I found no biography, interview, or other credits.

### Cited Findings — Doug Rasheed
- Described as "a Grammy-nominated, multi-platinum selling music producer, arranger and author"; profile lists a Grammy nomination and a BMI award; worked on records by Whitney Houston, Brandy, LL Cool J, New Edition, Jamie Foxx, Montell Jordan, Chaka Khan; credited with producing Coolio's "Gangsta's Paradise" and Tupac Shakur's "Only God Can Judge Me" — [Stage 32 profile](https://www.stage32.com/profile/1151308/about); [Society 61 Foundation](https://society61.org/team/doug-rasheed); [dougrasheed.com](https://www.dougrasheed.com/)
- Rate Your Music lists 43 credits (producer, songwriter, writer, composer, mixing engineer), including 1995 producer credits on Coolio's "Gangsta's Paradise" and Montell Jordan's "This Is How We Do It" — [Rate Your Music credits](https://rateyourmusic.com/artist/doug_rasheed/credits/)
- Discogs artist page notes he married Chaka Khan in 2001 (her third husband) — [Discogs: Doug Rasheed](https://www.discogs.com/artist/173757-Doug-Rasheed)
- Background per Rolling Stone's oral history: Rasheed was roommates with Coolio's manager Paul Stewart; the "Gangsta's Paradise" hook is lifted from Stevie Wonder's 1976 "Pastime Paradise" — [Rolling Stone oral history](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/coolios-gangstas-paradise-the-oral-history-of-1995s-pop-rap-smash-50357/)

## Key Question 3 — Which engineer(s) recorded/mixed the track, who mastered the album, executive producer, session musicians, background vocals?

### Takeaway
Album-level credits name Dave Aron, Tommy "D" Daugherty, Rick Clifford, Carlos Warlick and David Blake (DJ Quik) as engineers/mixers, Alvin McGill as second engineer, and Brian "Big Bass" Gardner as mastering engineer; Suge Knight was executive producer. I found no reliable per-track engineer credit for "Only God Can Judge Me" — one AI-generated encyclopedia (Grokipedia) attributes it to Dave Aron and dates it October 28, 1995, but that is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- Album personnel per Wikipedia: Brian Gardner — mastering; Dave Aron, Rick Clifford, Tommy Daugherty — engineers; Carlos Warlick and David Blake — mixing; recorded at Can-Am Studios, Tarzana, Los Angeles, October 13 – December 18, 1995 — [Wikipedia: All Eyez on Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Eyez_on_Me); [culture.fandom mirror](https://culture.fandom.com/wiki/All_Eyez_on_Me)
- Qobuz credits list Brian "Big Bass" Gardner as mastering engineer on multiple tracks and Alvin McGill as "recording second engineer" — [Qobuz: All Eyez On Me](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/all-eyez-on-me-2pac/yz65mj50gv0uc)
- Dave Aron: began engineering at Can-Am Studios in 1993 and started a long affiliation with Death Row; served as engineer and mixer on *All Eyez on Me*; has mixed all of Snoop Dogg's albums — [Wikipedia: Dave Aron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Aron)
- Grokipedia (AI-generated, low reliability) states the track was recorded October 28, 1995 at Can-Am Studios, Tarzana, "engineered by Dave Aron," and describes it as "a straightforward G-funk beat with simple loops, percussion, and a heavy drum pattern" with synth-driven elements — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Only_God_Can_Judge_Me)
- Discogs lists a "Death Row Engineers" collective comprising Rick Clifford, Tommy D. Daugherty, Alvin McGill, Dave Aron and Keston Wright — [Discogs: Death Row Engineers](https://www.discogs.com/artist/3915394-Death-Row-Engineers)
- Kelley Library catalog record for the album notes "Recorded at Can-Am Studios" — [Kelley Library record](https://discover.kelleylibrary.org/Record/480002)
- Executive producer / bail: Suge Knight posted Tupac's $1.4 million bail in October 1995 pending appeal and signed him to Death Row; Interscope's David Cohen later said Interscope and Time Warner actually put up the money — [Suge Knight (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suge_Knight); [Ambrosia For Heads: Iovine/Interscope bail](https://ambrosiaforheads.com/2017/07/interscope-bailed-tupac-not-death-row/); [Diverse Mentality](https://diversementality.com/tupac-death-row-contract/)
- Tommy "D" Daugherty: Death Row house engineer who recorded/mixed for Dr. Dre, Snoop Dogg and 2Pac; previously worked with Prince and George Clinton; came from remix work with Keith Cohen at Larrabee West — [Tape Op interview](https://tapeop.com/interviews/150/tommy-d-daugherty); wrote tell-all *Stranded on Death Row*; described as RIAA Diamond-certified engineer — [AllHipHop](https://allhiphop.com/news/death-rows-chief-engineer-writes-tell-book-label/); directory lists him as Death Row senior audio engineer 1992–2008 (low-reliability aggregator) — [ZoomInfo](https://www.zoominfo.com/p/Tommy-Daugherty/5354686258); Discogs discography — [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/artist/337158-Tommy-D-Daugherty); recalled the "California Love" Can-Am session — [Wikipedia: California Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Love)
- Brian "Big Bass" Gardner: born Brian Knapp Gardner, from Idaho; started 1965 in RCA Studios Hollywood mastering room (Creedence, Jefferson Airplane); later Fantasy Studios and Allen Zentz Mastering; joined Bernie Grundman Mastering in 1984 and retired in 2015; nickname "Big Bass" given by Dr. Dre; longtime second engineer Mike Bozzi — [Wikipedia: Brian Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Gardner); [Discogs: Brian Gardner](https://www.discogs.com/artist/70778-Brian-Gardner); [Mix: retirement](https://www.mixonline.com/the-wire/brian-big-bass-gardner-retires-bernies-after-40-years-mastering-425890); [Mix Q&A](https://www.mixonline.com/recording/brian-big-bass-gardner-qa-366242); [Wikipedia: Mike Bozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Bozzi)
- Low-quality SEO pages name "Dave Aron and Rick Clifford" as the album's engineers; excluded as unreliable (e.g., library.peoplespharmacy.com/2pac-eyez-me-cd).

### Inferences
- Since Dave Aron was Can-Am's in-house engineer with the longest Death Row tenure and is the only name any source attaches to this track, he is the most probable tracking/mix engineer — but this remains an inference pending the booklet or Discogs per-track credits.
- Mastering at Bernie Grundman Mastering by Brian Gardner is album-wide and therefore applies to this track.

- No source found for per-track credits on "Only God Can Judge Me" for: engineer, mixer, keyboards/bass/session musicians, background vocals, or Lance Pierre / Kevin Lewis (names in the brief that returned zero results in connection with this album).

## Key Question 4 — Does the song sample anything? What does WhoSampled list?

### Takeaway
WhoSampled lists exactly one sample in the song — the drums of Audio Two's "Top Billin'" (1987), entering at 0:15 and running throughout. A 2PacLegacy claim that it samples Kleeer's "Tonight" is contradicted (that sample belongs to "California Love").

### Cited Findings
- "Only God Can Judge Me" contains a sample of "Top Billin'" by Audio Two (drum loop; sample appears at 0:15 and throughout) — [WhoSampled sample entry](https://www.whosampled.com/sample/5094/2Pac-Rappin'-4-Tay-Only-God-Can-Judge-Me-Audio-Two-Top-Billin'/); [WhoSampled song page](https://www.whosampled.com/2Pac/Only-God-Can-Judge-Me/)
- WhoSampled's song page counts 9 later songs sampling it and lists no covers — [WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/2Pac/Only-God-Can-Judge-Me/)
- 2PacLegacy has a post titled "2Pac – Only God Can Judge Me Was Sampled Of 'Tonight' by Kleeer" — [2PacLegacy](https://2paclegacy.net/2pac-only-god-can-judge-me-was-sampled-of-tonight-by-kleeer/); contradicted by a Kleeer *Intimate Connection* reissue listing, which says "California Love" (2Pac and DJ Quik) sampled "Tonight" — [Acoustic Sounds: Kleeer](https://store.acousticsounds.com/d/150966/Kleeer-Intimate_Connection-Vinyl_Record), and by WhoSampled, which does not list Kleeer for this track

### Inferences
- The 2PacLegacy "Kleeer" post most likely conflates this song with "California Love"; the report should rely on WhoSampled's "Top Billin'" drum sample as the only documented sample.

### Gaps
- Could not open WhoSampled to read sample-type details (e.g., whether "Top Billin'" is flagged as direct sample vs. replay) or the full list of 9 sampling songs.

## Key Question 6 — Release details, studio/dates, single/video status, charts, certification, soundtracks, later releases

### Takeaway
The track was never an official single or video; it appears on *All Eyez on Me* (Death Row/Interscope, Feb 13, 1996; recorded at Can-Am Oct 13–Dec 18, 1995), was NOT on the *Gang Related* soundtrack, but was later included on *The Hate U Give* soundtrack (2018), the 2017 biopic *All Eyez on Me*, and the bootleg compilation *The Prophet: The Best of the Works*.

### Cited Findings

### Takeaway

Too Short* (1988), founded Rag Top Records in 1991, had two Hot 100 top-40 hits on Chrysalis/EMI ("Playaz Club" #36, 1994; "I'll Be Around," 1995), produced with Doug Rasheed on *Off Parole* (1996), describes his bond with Tupac as "a brother thing" and this track as a career-defining moment; he has had drug-related prison time and a 2017 arrest, and since March 2024 has been fighting multiple myeloma while mentoring youth.
